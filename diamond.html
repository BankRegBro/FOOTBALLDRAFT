How to Play
Diamond Kings · All-Era Roster Draft
The Drill
  • Every roll locks in a franchise and a decade. You draft what the dice give you, with no takebacks.
  • Pick one player from that team and era into any open spot on your roster.
  • Fill all thirteen spots: the nine fielders on the diamond, plus a manager, a no.2 starter, a closer, and a designated hitter in the dugout.
  • The three outfield spots take any outfielder; the DH takes any everyday bat; every other spot takes only its own position.
  • When the roster is full, it grades out of 100 and projects to a 162-game record and a postseason finish.
  • You cannot draft the same player twice out of the same decade.
What's Scored
  • Spots are weighted. The ace starter counts the most. Shortstop and center field carry a small premium for up-the-middle value; the DH counts a touch less.
  • There is no position-fit bonus or penalty: a player rates the same wherever they are eligible to play.
  • Every roster is built from real standouts, so the raw average always runs high. The club rating stretches that band onto a full 40–100 scale, which is why a blind draft lands near .500 and a sharp one climbs toward a champion.
  • If a rolled franchise and decade has no one who fits an open spot, the dice never land there, so you are never stuck waiting on a team that cannot help.
Modes & the Hall of Fame
  • Era sets the dice: All-Era rolls every decade from the 1970s on, 90s+ starts at the 1990s, 2000s+ starts at the 2000s.
  • Difficulty: Blitz hides ratings while you draft. Rookie marks elite players with a star as you pick. The final score is identical either way; Blitz is simply blind.
  • The Hall of Fame records Blitz, All-Era runs only. It shares one global board with the rest of the arcade. Any other setup still plays and scores, but will not post.
